ChiLog

A pet health tracking app concept built for speed and clarity

I designed the end-to-end UX and UI to help pet owners log daily health activities quickly, see patterns over time, and arrive at vet visits with a clear summary.

01

Design Highlight

Health Timeline: making patterns readable

Separate charts looked clean but made it difficult to compare categories—for example, whether diarrhea and a skipped meal occurred on the same day.

The final timeline aligns categories on a shared date axis, using dot counts for meals and poo, number badges for pee frequency, and larger dots with count badges for symptoms.

02

Problem

Pet owners struggle to track everyday health consistently. Logging takes too long, information is scattered, and caregivers lack a shared record—making it hard to recall symptoms at the vet or know whether medication has already been given.

Logging friction

Too many steps turn quick observations into a task owners put off.

Scattered information

Notes, messages, and memory don’t provide a reliable health history at a vet visit.

Caregiver blind spots

Without shared records, caregivers can miss a dose or accidentally give it twice.

03

Research

Survey & competitive analysis

I surveyed pet owners across different lifestyles and pet types. All respondents relied on memory, notes, or messaging apps. Quick logging, vet summaries, and reminders were the three most requested features.

Across the five competitors reviewed, none offered a free vet summary. I identified gaps in quick logging, organized records, and cross-category health visualization.

04

Define

Jobs to be done

The daily logger

Po · one dog · shared caregiver

When I notice a symptom, skipped meal, or unusual behavior, I want to record it immediately so I can build an accurate history without relying on memory.

Design target: log in under five seconds, without mandatory typing.

The vet preparer

Shelly · two cats · shared caregiver

Before a vet visit, I want a summary of what has happened since the last appointment so I can answer questions accurately.

Design target: a one-tap summary to view or screenshot in the waiting room.

Design opportunities

  • Make logging fast enough to happen in the moment.
  • Let caregivers see whether medication has already been given.
  • Surface patterns without making owners search through logs.
  • Provide a clear health snapshot before a vet visit.
05

IA & User Flow

Information architecture

I mapped the app structure before designing screens, giving each feature a clear place in the navigation.

Log medication (2 taps)

The shortest path for a high-stakes daily action: open the reminder and record the dose.

Log a poo entry (3 taps)

Button-based conditions and optional notes reduce typing. Owners can flag an abnormal entry for the vet summary and optionally attach a photo, without creating a separate symptom record.

Vet visit summary (2 taps)

The summary uses existing logs, with “Since last visit” as the default date range. No extra input is required.

Evaluation

What I learned

  • Remove work before adding features. Minimum mandatory fields and a “Flag this log” option keep everyday logging lightweight.
  • Choose visualization around the task. A shared timeline better supports preparing for a vet visit than separate category charts.
  • Structural decisions compound. Separating logs from schedules and making pet context global helped keep screens consistent.

What I would validate next

This concept has not yet undergone formal usability testing. I would observe pet owners using the flag option and timeline for the first time, then review the vet summary with veterinarians to understand what information is most useful during a consultation.
