01

Problem

Building a Digital Home for a Growing Community

As new card releases expanded the physical game, players needed a shared place to manage collections, build decks, and discover other players. Five Mics Central connects those activities with player profiles and event activity.

Competitive Research

What I looked at:

What I learned:

01 — Fragmented Ecosystems

Players often switch between separate tools for decks, events, rankings, and community.

02 — Powerful, but Hard to Use

Dense interfaces and steep learning curves made clear hierarchy and approachable interactions a priority.

03 — Community as Part of the Product

Linking decks, profiles, events, and leaderboards helps players discover each other and share strategies.

03

Turning Extra Cards into a Resale Loop

I proposed a Sell Cards flow that connects duplicate cards in players’ collections to Five Mics’ review process and Shopify storefront.

I built the front-end state logic for editing, pricing, validating, and submitting multiple cards:

  1. SelectChoose cards from Collection
  2. ConfigureCondition + Variant
  3. PriceMatch with Supabase card data
  4. ValidateEnsure every selected card is complete
  5. SubmitSend sell request to Five Mics
  6. Review & ListApproved cards received → Shopify

Technical Challenge

Keeping collection data, per-card attributes, pricing, validation, and cart state synchronized across multiple selected cards.

07

Evaluation

From Concept to Launch

Taking Five Mics Central from concept to launch taught me to balance product architecture, launch scope, and implementation constraints. Building the product also revealed opportunities—such as the resale flow—that were not part of the initial brief.
