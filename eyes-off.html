Eyes Off!

An interactive storytelling game exploring social pressure and victim-blaming.

I designed and developed a nine-level puzzle game that turns everyday social expectations into a playable journey from conformity to resistance.

01

Motivation

In 2022, an anonymous account of suspected secret photography went viral. Instead of receiving support, the woman faced victim-blaming that shifted attention away from the suspected harassment.

I researched gender theory and personal stories to explore how a game could challenge the expectations placed on people who experience harm.

02

Research Question

Drawing on Raewyn Connell’s Gender in World Perspective and Erinn Cunniff Gilson’s work on vulnerability and victimization, I explored the relationship between social judgment, vulnerability, and agency.

How can embracing vulnerability inspire agency and disrupt victim-blaming culture?
03

Design Process

My early storyboard used a zoom-out reveal: an apparently neutral scene took on gendered meaning as the wider context exposed social expectations. Playtesting showed that the reveal offered too little for players to do.

I introduced Peepers, an eye monster embodying social judgment, and reworked the scenarios into interactive puzzles. Giving players a visible antagonist turned an idea to interpret into a conflict they could act on.

04

Final Level Design

I selected nine scenarios from the storyboards and organized them into three chapters, moving from abstract social pressure to gender expectations and specific experiences of harm.

General: introducing social gaze

Gender roles: confronting expectations

Harms: making vulnerability visible

In the first playthrough, players adjust red characters to fit social expectations. Characters turn green and Peepers close their eyes, making puzzle completion visible. Increasing complexity sets up the contrast between conformity and resistance.

05

Game Mechanics

First, players solve puzzles by fitting social expectations. On the second playthrough, the Eye-Bye Glove lets them revisit the same scenes and slap Peepers’ eyes away—challenging the judgment instead of changing themselves.

The glove’s gray box appears in every level from the start: the option to resist was always there.

Unity Development

I designed, illustrated, animated, and developed the game in Unity. I used the FEEL plug-in for animation feedback and camera movement, while Peepers’ sound and motion reinforced the tension of being watched.

Exhibition

09

Evaluation

Players responded particularly to slapping Peepers and rejecting judgment. Their interpretations varied, but remained connected to the theme of social change.

What I would explore next

  • Expand the scenarios to represent a wider range of everyday experiences.
  • Develop more varied interactions while preserving the contrast between conformity and resistance.
  • Test accessibility and comprehension with players across age groups.
