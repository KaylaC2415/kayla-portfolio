Light It Out

A flashlight-controlled indie arcade game

A school prototype developed into a physical arcade experience exhibited at MAGFest 2023. I led game design, Unity development, and exhibition production in a three-person team.

01

Ideation

What if players mirrored the actions of the in-game character?

I explored player-character synchrony through a flashlight controller powered by Arduino and photoresistors. Players use real light to navigate a dark, first-person maze.

I built a low-fidelity Unity prototype to test the interaction. Learning Arduino through trial and error let me turn the idea into a playable experience, which received positive feedback from playtesters.

02

Narrative & Game Mechanics

I developed the narrative, mechanics, and player flow around one tension: use light to find your way, but risk being caught in the process.

Narrative

Play as John, a security guard navigating a surreal maze to find the yellow door and escape.

Game mechanics

  • Illuminate paths to explore the maze.
  • Hide in darkness when footsteps approach to avoid capture.

Player flow & storyboard

Light and sound create a loop of exploration, risk, and retreat. I translated the player flow into a storyboard to align the team on pacing, instructions, and narrative moments.

04

Exhibition at MAGFest 2023

At MAGFest, players gathered around the arcade, recorded their sessions, and discussed the flashlight controller. Their responses highlighted the appeal of the physical interaction and the story’s ending.

Resetting an unattended arcade

Players sometimes walked away after finishing or left a session midway through. The original idle timeout of about one minute kept the next visitor waiting at an unfinished game.

I shortened the automatic return to the home screen to 30 seconds of inactivity, balancing a brief pause during play with a faster reset for the next player.
